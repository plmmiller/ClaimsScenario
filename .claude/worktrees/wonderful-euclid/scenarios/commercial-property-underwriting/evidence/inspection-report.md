## Hazard Assessment

### Internal Hazards

#### 1. Repair Area (Priority: Moderate)
The repair area occupies approximately 800 sq ft in the northeast corner of the premises. Equipment observed includes:
- 3 soldering stations (temperature-controlled soldering irons)
- 2 heat guns (for component rework and adhesive removal)
- Fume extraction system (ducted to exterior)
- Magnification lamps and diagnostic equipment
- Small parts bins with electronic components

The area has dedicated exhaust ventilation, a rate-of-rise heat detector, sprinkler coverage, and both an ABC and CO2 fire extinguisher. Work surfaces are ESD-safe laminate (fire-retardant). **Note:** Heat-producing equipment in use. Recommend confirming hot work procedures and verifying that combustible materials are stored away from heat sources. During inspection, no combustible storage was observed within 10 feet of soldering stations.

#### 2. Inventory Storage (Priority: Low-Moderate)
The rear storage area (approximately 3,000 sq ft) contains retail inventory on metal shelving units. Inventory consists primarily of boxed electronics in manufacturer packaging (cardboard and Styrofoam). Storage is organized and does not exceed sprinkler clearance requirements (minimum 18 inches maintained below sprinkler deflectors). **Note:** High-value inventory is susceptible to water and smoke damage.

#### 3. Lithium Batteries (Priority: Low)
Standard retail quantities of lithium-ion batteries observed in inventory (phone batteries, laptop batteries, portable chargers). No bulk battery storage. Quantities are consistent with a retail electronics operation and do not represent an elevated fire hazard beyond normal retail electronics occupancy.

### External Hazards

#### 4. Adjacent Restaurant - Casa Verde Mexican Restaurant (Priority: Moderate-High)
Full-service restaurant with commercial kitchen located in the adjacent unit to the east. Observed during exterior walk-around:
- Commercial grease exhaust hood and ductwork on the shared east exterior wall
- Grease trap located at ground level on the shared exterior wall
- Kitchen exhaust fan running during inspection
- Hood cleaning certificate posted (Alliance Fire Protection, cleaned January 2026, next due July 2026)

**Fire wall assessment:** The demising wall between TechVault and Casa Verde is 2-hour rated CMU construction. This was confirmed by review of the building permit (Travis County Permit #2001-CB-44721). No penetrations through the fire wall were observed from the TechVault side. The fire wall extends from floor to roof deck.

**Note:** The restaurant represents the most significant adjacent occupancy exposure. While the 2-hour fire wall provides substantial separation, a commercial kitchen fire that extends to the shared roof structure could affect the insured premises. Hood cleaning certificate is current. Recommend verifying hood cleaning frequency (NFPA 96 requires quarterly for high-volume cooking operations).

#### 5. Vacant Adjacent Unit - Suite 110 (Priority: Moderate)
The adjacent unit to the west (4,500 sq ft) has been vacant for approximately 6 months. Exterior observations:
- Rear windows boarded with plywood (two of three)
- Small accumulation of debris (cardboard, food wrappers) near rear entrance
- Front entrance secured with standard lock
- Utilities reported maintained by building management
- No "For Lease" signage observed (landlord representative confirmed actively marketing)

**Note:** Vacant units present increased risk for vandalism, trespass, and arson. The debris accumulation at the rear could attract pests or serve as ignition material. Recommend requesting confirmation from building management regarding security monitoring and maintenance schedule for vacant unit.

#### 6. Fire Lane Access (Priority: Low-Moderate)
The fire lane along the front of the strip mall was partially blocked by two delivery vehicles during the inspection (11:30 AM). Store manager confirmed that delivery trucks routinely park in the fire lane during morning hours (approximately 10 AM - 1 PM, 3-4 days per week). This could delay fire department access.

## Water Damage History Assessment

Two ceiling tiles in the rear storage area (approximately 40 feet from the south wall) show faint water staining. The staining pattern is consistent with the June 2023 water damage claim described in the loss history. The stains appear dry and historical -- no moisture was detected using a handheld moisture meter at the time of inspection. The ceiling tiles have not been replaced. The roof area above these tiles was inspected and no active moisture intrusion was identified.

**Finding:** The water staining appears to be residual from the 2023 loss event and does not indicate ongoing moisture intrusion. However, the tiles should be replaced to allow for future moisture monitoring. Any new staining would indicate a recurrence.

---

## Recommendations

### Priority 1 (Required - Complete within 30 days of binding)
1. **Replace stained ceiling tiles** in rear storage area to establish clean baseline for moisture monitoring.
2. **Confirm hot work procedures** for repair area. Written procedures should document safe operating practices for soldering stations and heat guns, including cool-down protocols and end-of-day shutoff verification.

### Priority 2 (Recommended - Complete within 90 days)
3. **Verify restaurant hood cleaning frequency** meets NFPA 96 requirements for high-volume cooking (quarterly minimum).
4. **Address vacant unit debris accumulation** with building management. Request regular exterior maintenance of vacant unit.
5. **Address fire lane obstruction** - coordinate with building management to establish designated delivery zones away from fire lane.

### Priority 3 (Suggested - Ongoing)
6. **Maintain quarterly roof drain cleaning schedule** - document each cleaning with dated records.
7. **Annual roof inspection** by qualified roofing contractor (in addition to insurance inspection).
8. **Relocate high-value inventory** away from areas directly below roof penetrations and HVAC curbs to minimize water damage exposure.
