## Written Statement

I, Linda S. Park, provide the following voluntary statement regarding the traffic collision I witnessed on March 15, 2026.

I live at 782 Elm Avenue, which is about two blocks east of the intersection of Oak Street and Elm Avenue. At approximately 2:45 PM on Saturday, March 15, 2026, I was walking my dog, a golden retriever named Biscuit, along the north sidewalk of Elm Avenue heading westbound toward the intersection.

As I approached the intersection, I was at the northeast corner waiting at the crosswalk. I was looking down at my dog because he was sniffing at something on the ground near the curb. I was not looking at the traffic or the traffic signals at that moment.

I suddenly heard a loud screeching sound, which I now believe was the sound of tires braking on the road. I immediately looked up toward the intersection. I saw two cars in the process of colliding. A silver car was in the middle of the intersection, turned at an angle like it was making a left turn. A dark blue car was heading westbound on Elm Avenue and was hitting the silver car on its right front side.

The collision happened very fast. After the initial impact, both cars slid forward and to the left (from my perspective) and came to rest in the westbound lanes of Elm Avenue, past the intersection.

When I looked up after hearing the brakes, I glanced at the traffic signal. The light facing the Elm Avenue direction appeared to be green. However, I want to be completely honest -- I was not watching the signal before the collision. I only noticed the light was green after I heard the screeching and looked up. I cannot say with certainty what the signal was showing at the exact moment the silver car entered the intersection to make its turn.

What I can say is that the silver car appeared to be turning kind of fast. It did not look like a slow, cautious turn. It seemed like the driver was trying to make the turn quickly, almost like they were trying to beat something, but I cannot be sure about that.

After the collision, I immediately called 911. My call was placed at 2:48 PM according to my phone. I stayed at the scene until the police and ambulances arrived. I saw both drivers get out of their vehicles (the man from the dark blue car needed help getting out). The woman from the silver car was holding her neck and seemed to be in pain. The man from the blue car was limping and could not put weight on his right leg.

I did not see anyone else witness the collision, although there were other cars at the intersection that had stopped.

I was approximately 40-50 feet from the point of impact when the collision occurred, at the northeast corner crosswalk.

The weather was clear and sunny. The road was dry. There was nothing blocking my view of the intersection from where I was standing.

## Additional Questions and Answers

**Q: Have you ever witnessed a traffic collision before?**
A: No, this is the first one.

**Q: Were you wearing glasses or contacts at the time?**
A: I wear prescription glasses for distance. I was wearing them at the time.

**Q: Did you see either vehicle before the collision?**
A: I may have seen the blue car approaching from my right (eastbound direction -- correction, the blue car was going westbound, so it would have been coming from my left). I'm not really sure. I wasn't paying attention to traffic because I was focused on my dog.

**Q: Did you hear anything before the screeching tires?**
A: No, nothing unusual. Just normal traffic sounds.

**Q: How long after the screeching did the collision occur?**
A: Almost immediately. Maybe one or two seconds at most. It was very fast.

**Q: Can you estimate the speed of either vehicle?**
A: The blue car seemed to be going normal speed for that road. Not super fast, not slow. The silver car was turning, so it was going slower but it still seemed like a quick turn to me.

**Q: Did you notice the condition of the traffic signals at any point before the collision?**
A: No. As I said, I was looking at my dog. I only noticed the green light for Elm Avenue after I heard the brakes and looked up.

**Q: Is there anything else you would like to add?**
A: I just want to help however I can. It was a scary thing to see. I hope both drivers are okay. I want to be truthful -- I believe the blue car had the green light, but I cannot be 100% sure since I wasn't watching the signal before everything happened.

## Follow-Up Statement Addendum

**Date:** March 22, 2026
**Taken By:** Claims Investigator Rachel Simmons, Pacific Coast Insurance
**Method:** Phone interview

During a follow-up phone interview, Ms. Park reiterated her account consistent with her initial statement. When asked additional questions about the scene, she provided the following:

- She confirmed she lives in the neighborhood and walks her dog along this route "almost every day."
- She is familiar with the signal timing at this intersection and stated that "the left turn arrow for Oak Street usually only lasts about 10-15 seconds."
- When asked if she recalled seeing a green arrow versus a solid green for Oak Street traffic, she stated she did not see the Oak Street signal at all, only the Elm Avenue signal after the collision.
- She mentioned that she recognized the driver of the blue car. She said, "Actually, now that I think about it, that man looks familiar. He might be one of my nephew Kevin's coworkers. Kevin works at a warehouse logistics company in West Sacramento. I think I might have seen that man at Kevin's birthday party last year, but I'm not sure. It's a small world." She stated this did not affect her statement in any way and she provided her account honestly regardless.
- She declined to change any portion of her original statement.
